## THE ORIGIN

Prompt Party started as a personal development goal.

I'd been incorporating AI tools into my own design workflow — using Claude for research synthesis, building with Claude Code, prompting my way through problems I used to solve manually. The more I explored, the more I found that the barrier wasn't the tools themselves. It was not knowing where to start, and not having anyone to figure it out with.

I proposed a solution to my manager: a biweekly session where the SAP UX design team could explore AI-assisted design together. No pressure, no formal deliverables, no "correct" way to use it. Just practice, sharing, and building fluency over time.

She said yes. I designed the curriculum, built the supporting site with Claude Code, and hosted the first session in May 2026.

---

## THE CURRICULUM DESIGN

The 10-session curriculum isn't random. It maps the full design-to-handoff workflow — giving the team a way to practice AI-assisted design across every phase they already work in:

| Session | Topic | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build your profile card | Understand vibe coding without overthinking output |
| 2 | Wireframing: 3-screen flow | Sketch a user flow quickly without getting distracted by style |
| 3 | UX Research | Apply AI to research synthesis and insight generation |
| 4 | Hi-Fi Design | Style wireframes with real design tokens |
| 5 | Responsiveness | Make a design work across mobile and desktop |
| 6 | Interactivity & Motion | Add purposeful animation |
| 7 | A/B Variants | Build two versions to compare design decisions |
| 8 | Handoff Specs | Generate dev-ready annotated specs |
| 9 | Accessibility | Audit and fix previous prototypes |
| 10 | Presentation | Build a reusable one-pager for stakeholder reviews |

The format was intentionally open: participants could apply sessions to real work projects, or use them as a creative break. The goal was practice, not performance.

## WHAT'S HAPPENED SO FAR

**The extension from 20 to 30 minutes:**
The original sessions were designed to be short — 20 minutes. The hypothesis was that keeping it brief would lower the pressure and make attendance sustainable. After the first two sessions, the team fed back that 20 minutes wasn't enough. We extended to 30. The constraint that was supposed to make it accessible was actually cutting into the learning. Adapting was the right call.

**Session 3 — swapping in UX Research:**
The original session 3 was design tokens. But while working on my own projects, I'd been discovering how useful AI could be for research synthesis — pattern finding, insight generation, structuring interview data. It felt too valuable not to share immediately.

I swapped session 3 to UX research and shared a Claude research skill I'd been building for my own workflow — imperfect, still in progress. Not polished, not finished. The point was to show the team that sharing work-in-progress is part of how this community grows, not something to wait on until everything is ready.

**The reception:**
The team said it's fun. Attendance has stayed consistent. The energy in sessions has been good. Small signals, but meaningful ones for an internal initiative competing with everyone's actual workload.

---

## THE REAL GOAL

The official pitch was: build AI fluency across the UX design team.

The real goal is something more specific. Not to standardize how everyone uses AI — every designer has a different workflow, different strengths, different ways of working. What I'm hoping for is that each person finds *their* version of AI-assisted design. The sessions are a structured space to experiment. The community is what makes those experiments stick.

Sharing my own imperfect Claude research skill was a deliberate signal: I'm figuring this out too. We're doing this together.

## THE SITE

Built with Claude Code — a direct demonstration of the thing Prompt Party is teaching. The site itself is a case study in AI-assisted design: designed in conversation with Claude, built in the terminal, deployed to GitHub Pages.

The site includes a session guide and replays table, tool recommendations, a FigJam integration for live participation, and a chatbot in the corner — written in Elaine's voice, warm and self-aware:

*"Hi there, I know everyone has different experiences with vibe coding. I'm actually pretty new to this, but it'll be fun and motivating to do it together 🎉 See you at the party 🎈"*
