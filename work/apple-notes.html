Apple Notes Redesign

Apple Notes is on every iPhone. Advanced note-takers still leave for Notion.

Role

UX Research · UI Design
End-to-end, solo

Timeline

9 weeks · 2022

Team

Solo — guided by
Sally Chung & Ian Abinoja

Status

Concept

Platform

iOS · iPhone

Overview

Native reach. Advanced friction.

Apple Notes lives on every iPhone — no download, no setup, no friction at entry. That accessibility makes it the default for millions. But as note-taking habits grow more complex, the app doesn't grow with them.

Users who need more — structured notes, reusable templates, table control, fast paragraph manipulation — eventually reach for something else. The irony: those third-party apps are harder to learn, not easier. The opportunity isn't to become Notion. It's to close the gap before users feel the need to leave.

The Problem

Three ways Apple Notes fails the power user.

01

Confusing IA

The app's structure feels simple — but that simplicity works against users organizing complex notes. What's hidden is hard to find. What's visible isn't always what's needed.

02

Editing Restraints

Formatting tools exist, but customization is shallow. Users can't apply consistent styles, control table cells, or do anything beyond basic text formatting — limitations that slow down advanced workflows.

03

Unproductive Flow

Moving and reusing content is tedious. Selecting text word-by-word on mobile is frustrating. There's no template system. Notes can't be easily restructured once written.

Research & Insights

The market has the depth. Notes has the reach.

Competitor analysis across Notion, Bear, and Obsidian confirmed: advanced note-taking apps offer real power, but carry steep learning curves. Users described switching as "necessary but annoying." The frustration wasn't with Apple Notes' simplicity — it was with hitting an invisible ceiling.

A UX audit and journey mapping of the existing app surfaced the exact moments that ceiling appears: mid-sentence table edits, trying to reuse a structure from a previous note, formatting a long document on a phone screen. Each moment is small. Together, they push people out.

Key Decisions

Three choices that shaped the redesign.

The Decision

Should the formatting toolbar hide when the keyboard appears, or stay persistently visible?

Options Considered

Hide toolbar to maximize screen real estate · Keep toolbar anchored to the keyboard at all times

We chose — persistent toolbar

Hiding tools creates friction at exactly the wrong moment — mid-thought. Anchoring the toolbar to the keyboard means formatting is always one tap away, never a mode to enter and exit.

The Decision

How should users rearrange content in long notes on mobile?

Options Considered

Word/character selection with cut and paste · Paragraph-level drag-and-drop blocks

We chose — paragraph drag

Precise text selection on a phone screen is an exercise in frustration. Treating paragraphs as draggable blocks respects the mobile context — faster, less error-prone, and more satisfying to use.

The Decision

Should users be able to save and reuse note structures?

Options Considered

Notes rebuilt manually each time · Saveable templates with customizable styles applied as presets

We chose — templates + styles

Power users repeat the same structures — meeting notes, research logs, project trackers. Making those structures reusable eliminates setup time and pulls Notes into a workflow, not just a scratchpad.

Solution

Six changes. One throughline: nothing hidden, nothing wasted.

Approachability

The toolbar stays. Always.

Formatting tools now anchor to the keyboard and stay visible whenever you're typing. No hunting through menus. The full suite — bold, italic, lists, tables, paragraph styles — is present the moment you need it.

Productivity

Rearrange paragraphs in one hold.

Activate the Paragraph panel, then hold and drag to reorder. What used to take 30 seconds of selection and paste operations now takes two seconds. The structure of a note is no longer locked the moment you write it.

Power

Tables that actually do table things.

The new Table panel adds color fills, text formatting, and cell operations: Freeze, Merge, Split. It's not a spreadsheet — it's a table that behaves like one when you need it to, without feeling like one when you don't.

Reusability

Build once. Reuse always.

Save any note as a template. Apply custom styles — heading weights, text sizes, color accents — and store them as presets. Meeting notes, research logs, project briefs: start from structure, not from scratch.

Navigation

Long notes, short scroll.

Paragraphs can now be collapsed and expanded. Long notes stay manageable — you see the structure at a glance, then drill in where needed. The difference between a document you navigate and one you wade through.

Design System

New components. Familiar language.

Every new icon and UI component was derived directly from Apple's Human Interface Guidelines. Nothing invented for its own sake — each element earns its place by following the system it lives in.

Outcome

What a 9-week concept proves.

This was a concept project — no launch metrics, no A/B test. But it demonstrated something worth saying: the gap between Apple Notes and advanced note-taking apps isn't a product category gap. It's a series of small, fixable friction points.

The redesign showed that you don't need to rebuild the app to serve power users. You need to surface the tools they already expect, make reorganization feel effortless on mobile, and give them a reason to build inside Notes rather than around it.
