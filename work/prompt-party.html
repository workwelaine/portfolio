Prompt Party

Not a training program. A shared experiment in figuring out how AI fits into real design work — led by someone who's figuring it out alongside everyone else.

Role

Initiator · curriculum designer · host · site builder

Timeline

May – Sep 2026 · 10 sessions

Audience

~25 UX designers, researchers, graphic designers

Status

3 sessions complete · 7 remaining

I'd been using AI in my own workflow — Claude for research synthesis, Claude Code to build — and found that the real barrier wasn't the tools. It was not knowing where to start, and not having anyone to figure it out alongside. I proposed a fix to my manager: a biweekly session where the SAP UX team could explore AI-assisted design together. No pressure, no deliverables, no correct answer. She said yes.

The Design Challenge

How do you build AI fluency without turning it into another obligation?

Low pressure. Sessions had to feel optional and exploratory — competing with everyone's actual workload. Mandatory formats kill the curiosity that makes learning stick. This had to feel like a break, not a meeting.

Grounded in real work. Abstract AI education doesn't transfer. The curriculum needed to map to what designers actually do every day — research, wireframing, hi-fi, handoff — so every session was immediately applicable, not theoretical.

Room for imperfect work. If sessions only showcased polished outcomes, participants would feel behind rather than invited in. The culture had to make sharing works-in-progress feel like the point, not something to apologize for.

The Curriculum

Ten sessions. One arc: the full design-to-handoff workflow.

The sessions aren't random — they map every phase designers already work in, so participants can practice AI-assisted design at each stage of work they already do.

Sessions 1–5

01 — Profile card: understand vibe coding without overthinking output

02 — Wireframing a 3-screen flow: sketch quickly without getting distracted by style

03 — UX Research: apply AI to synthesis and insight generation

04 — Hi-Fi Design: style wireframes with real design tokens

05 — Responsiveness: make a design work across mobile and desktop

Sessions 6–10

06 — Interactivity & Motion: add purposeful animation

07 — A/B Variants: build two versions to compare design decisions

08 — Handoff Specs: generate dev-ready annotated specs

09 — Accessibility: audit and fix previous prototypes

10 — Presentation: build a reusable one-pager for stakeholder reviews

Key Decisions

Two early calls that changed how the series runs.

The Decision

Sessions were originally 20 minutes — short by design to lower the commitment barrier. After sessions 1 and 2, the team said it wasn't enough. Hold the constraint or adapt?

Options Considered

Hold at 20 min — trust the format · Extend to 30 min · Add optional overflow after sessions

Extended — the constraint was cutting into the learning

The hypothesis was that shorter sessions would lower the barrier. The signal said otherwise: participants wanted more, not less. The constraint that was supposed to make it accessible was limiting what people could take away. Adapting to that signal immediately was the right call.

The Decision

Session 3 was originally design tokens. Mid-series, I found a more immediately valuable topic: AI-assisted research synthesis. Follow the curriculum or change course?

Options Considered

Follow the curriculum — consistency matters · Swap to UX research — share what's valuable now · Add research as a bonus session

Swapped — and shared the imperfect skill live

I'd been building a Claude research skill for my own workflow — imperfect, still in progress. I shared it live, unpolished. Deliberate signal: sharing unfinished work is part of how this community grows. Not something to wait on until everything is ready.

The Site

Built with Claude Code — a live demonstration of what it's teaching.

The Prompt Party site was designed in conversation with Claude and built in the terminal with Claude Code, then deployed to GitHub Pages. Session guide, replay table, tool recommendations, FigJam integration for live participation. And a chatbot in the corner, written in Elaine's voice:

"Hi there, I know everyone has different experiences with vibe coding. I'm actually pretty new to this, but it'll be fun and motivating to do it together 🎉 See you at the party 🎈"

— The Prompt Party chatbot

So Far

Three sessions in. The signals are good.

Attendance has stayed consistent. The team extended from 20 to 30 minutes based on their own feedback. The energy in sessions has been good — small signals, but meaningful for an internal initiative competing with everyone's actual workload.

The goal isn't to standardize how everyone uses AI. It's to help each person find their own version of AI-assisted design. The sessions create the space. The community is what makes the experiments stick. Seven sessions remain through September 2026.
