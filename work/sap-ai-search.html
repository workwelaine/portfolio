AI Search

The question wasn't whether AI would change how users find products. It was whether users were ready for the leap — and what to do with the space in between.

The Origin

The starting point wasn't "let's add AI." It was "this component is broken."

SAP Store had a guided product search — a tree-sorting component designed to help users navigate to the right product through a structured flow. In practice, three compounding problems made it unsustainable: the content was hardcoded with no CMS, so it drifted out of date every time the product catalog changed. Because it was hardcoded, there was no tracking — no clicks, no exits, no path data. It was invisible to the organization. And nobody owned it consistently.

Leadership was ready to retire it. They had AI chat in mind as the replacement. But AI chat was already live on SAP Store — and we didn't assume it was working. We waited 6 months after launch before designing the bridge, deliberately gathering data on actual AI chat performance before committing to a direction.

The hypothesis going in: the leap from a structured guided flow to an open-ended conversational interface might be too large for most users to make on their own. A bridge experience was needed — something between "I know exactly what I want" and "talk me through it."

Validation

Five dimensions evaluated before committing to the direction.

Before designing, we stress-tested whether the bridge approach was actually the right call — checking five dimensions that would determine whether this was viable to build, maintain, and use.

Development effort

Low barrier. AI chat was already built and live. The org was actively investing to expand it as part of SAP's company-wide "All In on AI" strategy. Building on existing infrastructure, not starting from scratch.

Business maintenance

Solved by the nature of AI. Unlike the hardcoded guided search that required manual updates and had no clear owner, AI chat is dynamic. No ownership problem — no stale content problem either.

Content sourcing

The AI chat draws from the same catalog as SAP's product search. What users find through traditional search is the same pool the AI advisor draws from. Consistency is built in, not designed in.

The Design Challenge

How do you build a bridge that works for users at both ends of the spectrum?

We explored variants across the full range — from traditional-search-adjacent to completely AI-first — to find what actually met users where they were.

Variant 1 — Dual CTA

Same input field as traditional search, two buttons: one directs to search results, one triggers AI chat. Familiar entry point, two exit paths. Low commitment — the user chooses after they've already started typing.

Variant 2 — Toggle

Two separate input fields separated by a toggle — one for traditional search, one for AI chat. Makes the distinction explicit. Users choose their mode before they start.

Variant 3 — Quick selections

Predefined selections that trigger AI chat — a structured on-ramp to a conversational experience. Lowers the barrier for users who don't know how to prompt an open-ended AI.

What Shipped

A bridge experience on two surfaces. The AI is Joule — not mine.

Launched on SAP Store and Partner Finding.

The bridge experience launched on the SAP Store landing page — the primary entry point for product discovery — and extended to the Partner Finding landing page, applying the same pattern to partner discovery.

The design connects users to Joule — SAP's LLM conversational widget, built by a separate team — through an intentional entry point that doesn't assume users are ready for full AI chat, but creates a clear path for those who are. My role was the entry point, the interaction spectrum, and the bridge logic. Not Joule itself.

Pending

Launch engagement data — click-through rate on AI chat entry, adoption rate vs traditional search, user behavior from the first weeks live.

What's Next

A/B testing to answer the bigger question.

The next phase compares the bridge experience against traditional search — understanding which users convert through AI chat vs traditional search, and under what conditions. The goal isn't to prove one beats the other. It's to understand what each serves better.

The bigger question this project is answering: does AI chat replace traditional search, or does it serve a different user with a different intent? A user who knows exactly what they want behaves differently from a user still exploring. The goal isn't to pick one — it's to serve both better.
